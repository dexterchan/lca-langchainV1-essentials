# Stories from Lantern Harbor

All people, albums, places, and genre histories in this document are fictional.
The matching catalog facts are in `music_catalog.db`.

## Mira Vale and Evening Lines

Mira Vale first sang for passengers waiting for the last ferry at Lantern Harbor.
She learned piano from a retired harbor mechanic who kept an upright piano in his workshop.
Her early songs left room for silence because the listeners were tired after long shifts.

Mira made *Evening Lines* after the ferry terminal closed for repairs.
She wanted to preserve the calm conversations that once filled its waiting room.
The record pairs a soft voice with piano and brushed drums.
Its opening song, *Last Ferry*, recalls the moment when the harbor lights appeared on the water.
*Window Seat* follows a traveler who chooses to stay home for one more evening.
The album suits quiet listening and gentle reflection.

## Harbor Jazz and Noah Reed

Harbor Jazz grew from small evening gatherings beside the ferry terminal.
Local players mixed jazz improvisation with the steady rhythm of dock work.
Mira's quiet piano songs represent its gentler side.

Noah Reed began as a street singer near the harbor's night market.
He shared the same musical roots, but preferred busy rhythms and bright horns.
His album *City Lights* captures the market after sunset.
*Market Steps* follows the movement between stalls.
*Neon Walk* describes the energy of friends walking home together.
The album suits an active evening and listeners who prefer lively jazz.

## Lea Moss and Workshop Blues

Lea Moss repaired radios during the day and sang in a community workshop at night.
She wrote songs about neighbors who helped each other through difficult weeks.
Her voice carries a rough edge, while her guitar keeps a steady pace.

Workshop Blues began when those neighbors turned their stories into songs.
The style values direct words, warm guitar, and the feeling of starting again.
Lea's album *Morning Blues* tells that story through *First Shift* and *Open Door*.
The record suits listeners who want warmth and resolve rather than a quiet background.

## Kai Rowan and Shore Ambient

Kai Rowan recorded waves and wind along the harbor's empty eastern shore.
Instead of singing, Kai arranged those sounds around long keyboard tones.
The album *Quiet Shores* follows the tide in *Tide Glass* and *Slow Horizon*.
Its music changes slowly and leaves space for reading or rest.

Shore Ambient developed from these recordings of the coast.
The style avoids strong beats and spoken words.
It suits listeners who want a calm atmosphere without vocals.
